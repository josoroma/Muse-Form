# Drawing craft

## Read before ornamenting

Write a short visual brief: subject, action, audience, camera, light, palette, material, and negative space. Derive these from the user's reference rather than defaulting every subject to the same mascot. Decide which silhouette, gesture, or expression must survive at 240 pixels wide.

Use the drawing to communicate form in this order:

1. Gesture and balance: a line of action, weight-bearing contact, opposing masses.
2. Proportion and perspective: major volumes, their axes, and a consistent viewpoint.
3. Silhouette and overlap: near forms occlude far forms; avoid confusing tangencies.
4. Three to five underlying value families, joined by continuous halftones for realism: paper/light, local midtone, form shadow, contact dark, occasional reflected light.
5. Selective details: features that identify the subject, then sparse material marks.

Small lines cannot rescue an incorrect silhouette. Marks should turn with the form and vary in pressure; repeated hatch patterns must not blanket the whole figure. Leave generous areas of quiet paper and an intentional light-facing edge.

## Form, light, color, composition, perspective, depth

- **Form:** construct a skull, ribcage, pelvis, or limb as volumes, then express the visible surface with contours. A forearm tapers and rotates; it is not a uniform tube. A face is not an oval with symbols pasted on it.
- **Light:** choose one main direction. Model broad form shadows, soft terminators, reflected light, and short occlusion shadows in creases. Cast shadows must agree with the contact surface. Carry the same illumination across neighboring parts. Realistic skin requires continuous halftones and subtle material response; indiscriminate shiny gradients or flat shadow patches destroy its volume.
- **Color:** begin with a limited harmony. The realism corpus uses warm ivory paper and graphite/charcoal for anatomy; story plates add restrained ochre, russet, moss, and cool ambient shadows. Shift hue as well as value in colored shadows. Give the focal region the strongest useful contrast.
- **Composition:** set a dominant diagonal or curve, vary large and small masses, balance the focal point with negative space. Test the full motion envelope, not only the resting pose. Preserve margin at the action extreme.
- **Perspective:** use shared axes for eyes, ears, shoulders, joints, and props. Compress far-side features and foreshorten forms moving toward the viewer. Avoid stretching a flat path as a substitute for a substantial turn.
- **Depth:** arrange background, far limbs, body, near limbs, props, and foreground deliberately. Reduce background contrast and detail; near forms occlude far forms. Establish a soft atmospheric background and specific foreground contact shadows. Do not put equal sharpness or texture everywhere.

## Anatomy and facial acting

| Subject | Structural landmarks | Motion decisions |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Skull | Cranial vault, eye sockets, zygomatic arch, nasal opening, maxilla, jaw hinge | Study a small turn or reveal light planes; keep teeth and jaw mechanically connected. |
| Eye | Globe beneath wrapping lids, inner/outer canthus, iris occlusion, brow ridge | Upper lid travels farther in a blink; eyebrow leads thought; gaze remains clipped within the opening. |
| Ear | Helix, antihelix, concha, tragus, lobe | Show depth with nested cartilage planes; subtle head-follow or an explanatory contour pass is enough. |
| Expressions | Brow angle, lid opening, cheeks, nasolabial folds, lip corners, jaw | Happiness lifts cheeks; sadness raises inner brows; anger compresses brow; fear widens tense eyes; surprise releases jaw; wink compresses one cheek. Avoid moving only a mouth symbol. |
| Torso/stretch | Ribcage and pelvis counter-rotate, shoulder line, clavicle, waist compression | One side lengthens while the other compresses. Neck and shoulder remain attached. |
| Arm | Shoulder cap, upper-arm mass, elbow, radius/ulna rhythm, wrist narrowing | Rotate at the real joint; maintain forearm length; keep hand gesture aligned with its wrist. |
| Hand | Palm wedge, thumb saddle, four finger rays, knuckle arc, webbing | Count five digits; thumb has its own opposing plane. Fingers taper and bend in linked segments. Use occlusion to hide joint seams. |
| Leg | Hip mass, quadriceps, kneecap, tibial ridge, calf, malleoli | Preserve the load path through knee and ankle. A supported foot stays planted unless lift is the action. |
| Foot | Heel block, arch, ball, big-toe direction, descending toe lengths | Show the medial arch and distinct ankle levels. Toes should not be five identical beads. Heel lift pivots through the ball of the foot. |

The corpus is artistic construction practice, not an anatomical diagnostic reference. For a physically accurate educational or medical illustration, verify the necessary structures against appropriate primary references.

## Character families

- **Pirates:** give coat, scarf, boots, hat, and prop different materials and lag. Use readable hand contact with the telescope, rope, or treasure. A bent elbow and counterbalanced torso make a gesture credible.
- **Monsters:** define a consistent invented skeleton. Distinct head/torso ratios, limb proportions, silhouette, and facial asymmetry make a character memorable. Fur or scales follow volumes.
- **Worms:** build a continuous body centerline with controlled thickness, segment rhythm, contact patches, and a traveling compression wave. Keep the head expression readable. Preserve connectedness when the body bends.
- **Dead or zombie characters:** use posture, uneven hair, hollowed planes, patched clothing, and delayed reactions. For young audiences, avoid gore and let a gentle goal carry the scene.
- **Children's stories:** show desire, effort, and a readable emotional payoff within the available time. A firefly, flower, star, or shared object can connect poses. Avoid using unexplained visual noise as story.

## Realistic raster plates

Use references for subject construction and the chosen visual language; explicitly distinguish those roles in the prompt. A style reference can pull the model toward its pose, so state required viewpoint differences clearly. If several studies cover a foot, hand, or face, make the viewpoint or anatomical emphasis materially distinct.

Request a resolved drawing or painting, not an artificial photograph filter: credible volumes, continuous tonal transitions, a few sharp focal edges, lost edges where light or atmosphere permits, and purposeful texture. Fine marks should not become uniform noise. Preserve generous margins around fingers, props, and the intended motion envelope.

Inspect generated anatomy before detail impresses you. Count digits and trace each to its base. Check nail planes, thumb opposition, webbing, forearm taper, offset calf heads, and the knee/ankle load path. Muscle masses should blend beneath skin; reject spherical biceps, separate oval muscle patches, tubular limbs, bead toes, or repeated identical fingers. Fantasy creatures need a consistent invented skeleton and weight-bearing contacts.

A finished plate is not a separated animation rig. Keep PNG as the canonical still, and store deformation regions or later layers separately. Record exact prompts, input roles, generation method, revisions, and selected output. Preserve source images and discarded variants unless cleanup was requested.

## Per-asset visual gate

| Check | Inspect | Repair when it fails |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Anatomy and pose | Digit count, attachment, joint centers, proportions, foreshortening, supported weight; follow each limb across overlaps. | Correct the local structure or regenerate the pose. Do not hide errors with texture. |
| Light and volume | One believable key light, continuous form shading, consistent material highlights and cast shadows. | Repaint or regenerate contradictory light, flat patches, blown highlights, or muddy values. |
| Edges and cleanliness | Clean silhouette, deliberate sharp/lost edges, useful detail hierarchy; no halos, stray contours, false seams or all-over noise. | Refine the edge or simplify the texture; inspect against both dark and light surroundings if exported with alpha. |
| Composition and contact | Focal point reads at thumbnail size; margins survive motion; hands grip props and feet meet the ground. | Recompose, correct occlusion, or adjust contact/shadows before rigging. |
| Identity and action | Expression and silhouette match the brief; repeated views remain distinct; action does not change anatomy or material. | Revisit the brief, viewpoint, or motion method rather than polishing the wrong result. |

Compare the full-size still, thumbnail, and reference after each material revision. Note concrete defects and fixes. A self-assigned score cannot prove quality; unresolved visible faults remain faults regardless of the score. The film gate is in [motion and export](motion.md).

## SVG construction

Use a viewBox and explicit dimensions. Separate geometry and shading into meaningful groups. Prefix gradient, clip, mask, and pattern IDs with a study identifier. Give each file a useful title and description. Use local `defs`; no remote fonts, images, styles, or scripts in portable SVG output.

Prefer path contours over collections of ellipses for anatomical silhouettes. A small number of primitives is useful for construction but should not determine the finished appearance. Use thicker near silhouettes and softer secondary contours. Hatching directions should turn with the form and be clipped to their intended plane. Avoid blurred filters as a substitute for drawing shadows.

Keep transforms away from the geometry when possible: a rest-position wrapper can contain a separate animated joint group. Rotation pivots should agree with the visible anatomy. Each animated path morph must preserve path command topology; for major topology changes, author distinct poses and transition deliberately.

## Corpus prompt template

“Create an original [subject and pose] as a finished [graphite plate / painting / native SVG]. Input [reference ID] provides [observed anatomy or gesture]; input [style reference] provides [rendering language], without copying book layout or captions. Compose [viewpoint, framing, gesture, focal point, negative space]. Preserve [anatomical landmarks, proportions, digit count, contact]. Illuminate from [direction] using [form shadows, reflected light, cast/contact shadows]. Use [palette, materials, edge hierarchy, selective texture]. Keep [motion envelope and invariants]. Avoid [specific defects relevant to this subject]. Deliver [dimensions and format], exact prompt and provenance.”

Write the action separately: “[starting pose], [intention], [small action], [reaction], [return or final hold]. Use [cinemagraph / articulated layers / authored poses] because [visible motion requirement]. Pin [support contacts and attachment points]. Inspect [subject-specific extreme-pose risks]. Export [duration, resolution, frame rate] and link the still, composition, film, and source ID.”

For native SVG, add viewBox, semantic groups, self-contained definitions, and editable geometry. For realistic plates, request continuous tones and clean edge control rather than SVG path lists.
